How it fits together

The big picture

Three layers. The phone app does everything it can on the device. Firebase stores data and runs the server functions. Outside services do the jobs we can't: maps, routes, texts, and locating a phone that has no app.

Why connectors matter. Every outside service sits behind a connector with a fake ("mock") version. That's why the whole app runs today with no backend: flip one setting and the same screens talk to the real service.

Flow 1

Signing in

flowchart LR
  A["Phone number screen: country from the phone, number checked as a real mobile"] -- "Send code" --> B["Firebase Auth texts a 6-digit code"]
  B --> C["Code screen: fills itself from Messages, signs in on the 6th digit"]
  C -- "wrong code" --> D["Red boxes: N tries left. 5 wrong locks this number for 10 minutes"]
  D --> C
  C -- "right code" --> E["You're in: explains location, asks While Using only"]
  E --> F["Home"]

Outside service: Firebase Authentication sends the text. In development a fake accepts any number and any code. Before real use, sign-in has to move to Firebase's native phone verification (the plan's "step 0").

Flow 2 · the core of the app

A journey, from Home to Arrived

flowchart TD
  H["Home: full-screen map with a pull-up sheet"] --> W["Where to? Search in the sheet"]
  W -- "address lookup" --> G(["Phone's own geocoder"])
  W --> R["Route: 3 to 5 steps and an ETA"]
  R -- "walking route" --> O(["OSRM routing"])
  R --> ST["Start. First time only: allow location"]
  ST --> J[("journeys record created")]
  J -.-> F1["onJourneyStarted: tells contacts who opted in"]
  ST --> OW["On the way: turn banner, ETA, check-in countdown"]
  OW -- "location updates, sent in batches" --> LU[("locationUpdates")]
  LU --> WL["Guardians: Watch live"]
  OW --> CI{"Check-in due"}
  CI -- "I'm OK, even from the lock screen" --> OW
  CI -- "no answer" --> MC["Missed check-in"]
  MC -.-> F2["onMissedCheckIn: push or text to contacts"]
  OW --> SC{"Safety check: late, stopped or off route"}
  SC -- "no answer" --> ESC["Escalated"]
  ESC -.-> F3["onSafetyCheckEscalated: alert, journey stays live"]
  OW -- "within 100 m for 20 s" --> AR["Arrived: private walk feedback, location stops"]

Backstops on the server. The phone detects a missed check-in or an unanswered safety check first. If the phone is off or out of signal, two scheduled functions catch it anyway: detectMissedCheckIns every 2 minutes and escalateOverdueSafetyChecks every minute.

Flow 3

Getting help, from uneasy to emergency

flowchart LR
  U["Feeling uneasy? pill"] --> U1["Call guardian"]
  U --> U2["Fake call: a convincing incoming call"]
  U --> U3["Location shared more often for 15 minutes"]
  S["SOS button"] -- "hold 3 s" --> T1["Guardians alerted with live location"]
  S -- "hold 6 s" --> T2["Also offers 999 or 112, from the phone's region"]
  T1 --> X["10 seconds to cancel"]
  T1 --> SE[("sosEvents")]
  SE -.-> F["onSOSTriggered: push to contacts with the app, text to the rest"]
  T1 -- "no data signal" --> OFF["Queued on the phone. Text my contacts now opens Messages"]

Nobody is called automatically. Emergency mode offers to call the first trusted contact; the person decides.

Push or text. For every alert, contacts with the app get a push and everyone else gets a text through the SMS provider. A shared tracking link opens a web page served by getSharedJourney.

Flow 5 · where most outside services plug in

Basic phones: finding someone who has no app

A grandparent or a child with a basic phone can still be found, through their mobile operator, but only after they say yes by text. Nothing is locatable until the consent reaches ACTIVE.

flowchart TD
  A["Guardian adds Sam: name and number"] --> C1[("consents: PENDING_SMS")]
  C1 -.-> RC["requestConsentSms"]
  RC ==> SMS1(["SMS provider sends: reply YES or STOP"])
  SMS1 --> M["Sam's basic phone"]
  M -- "replies YES" --> SMS2(["SMS provider webhook"])
  SMS2 ==> IN["inboundConsentSms: checks the signature, reads the word"]
  IN --> C2[("SMS_APPROVED")]
  C2 ==> OPC(["Operator consent (CIBA)"])
  OPC -- "operator asks Sam itself" --> C3[("OPERATOR_PENDING")]
  C3 -- "approved" --> C4[("ACTIVE")]
  C4 --> FIND["Guardian taps Find"]
  FIND --> LM["locateMember: holds Sam? ACTIVE? market on? within the hourly limit?"]
  LM ==> OPL(["Operator: CAMARA Location Retrieval"])
  OPL --> CIRCLE["A circle on the map: approximate, never a dot"]
  LM --> AUD[("locateAudits: every lookup recorded")]
  M -- "texts STOP, any time" --> IN
  IN --> REV[("REVOKED")]
  REV -.-> OCR["onConsentRevoked: tidy up, text Sam to confirm"]
Rounded boxes are outside services Thick arrows are calls to them Dotted arrows are functions a database write sets off

Safe zones and asking for help

flowchart LR
  Z["Guardian draws a zone: 500 m to 5 km, up to 5"] --> SZ[("safeZones")]
  SZ -.-> OZ["onSafeZoneCreated: texts Sam once"]
  CS["checkSafeZones, every 15 minutes"] ==> V(["Operator: inside this circle? yes or no"])
  V -- "2 agreeing answers flip it" --> ZE[("zoneEvents")] --> PUSH["Sam reached School, approximate"]
  H["Sam texts HELP or rings our number"] ==> WH(["SMS or voice provider webhook"])
  WH --> HB["inboundConsentSms or inboundMemberCall"]
  HB --> AL["Every ACTIVE guardian: push and text with an approximate location"]
  HB --> BACK["Text back to Sam: who was told, and call 112 or 999"]

Status today. All of this runs end to end against fake operator and SMS services and the local database emulator. Going live needs an operator aggregator contract, SMS and voice providers for the UK and India, India's TRAI DLT registration, and a legal review (decision G3).
